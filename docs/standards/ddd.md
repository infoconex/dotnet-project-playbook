# STD-014 — Domain-Driven Design and Domain Modeling

The playbook follows [Microsoft's published DDD guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/architecture/microservices/microservice-ddd-cqrs-patterns/ddd-oriented-microservice) by default. This standard records a **Recommended, conditionally applicable adoption choice** and makes one responsibility independently assessable: **the domain model should faithfully express the business language, boundaries, behaviors, and consistency rules that justify Domain-Driven Design (DDD)**. DDD is not a required architectural template for every .NET project.

The [standards catalog](README.md) registers this permanent identifier; the [on-demand DDD guide](../ddd-guidance.md) provides non-normative examples and assessment questions. [Standards governance](../governance/standards.md) controls applicability and alternatives.

## <a name="std-014"></a>Purpose, level, and applicability

**Level: Recommended.** Prefer strategic and, where warranted, tactical DDD for new or materially changed areas with **meaningful domain complexity**: evolving business rules, important invariants, distinct business meanings for similar terms, complicated lifecycles, or context relationships that create costly misunderstanding or coupling. Adoption can apply to one bounded context without applying to every subsystem or project in a solution. Invest more deeply in strategically important core subdomains; supporting and generic subdomains may need simpler modeling or established products.

For straightforward CRUD, predominantly technical orchestration, or a small pure library with few rules and no conflicting business meanings, simple cohesive types, direct collaboration, and explicit validation may be enough. Declining DDD where its costs outweigh its value is a legitimate **documented alternative**, not a failure of a Required standard. Likewise, using an immutable domain value type does not by itself mean the whole solution has adopted DDD. Judge actual responsibilities rather than labels, project size, or framework versions.

Apply this recommendation proportionately to modern .NET and traditional .NET Framework; choose language constructs and infrastructure options supported by the actual compiler, target framework, project format, and tooling. Existing systems may adopt DDD incrementally around affected behavior while preserving externally visible compatibility. DDD neither requires nor implies microservices, a distributed transaction model, or additional compiled assemblies. Independently, [STD-017 — Microservices Architecture](microservices.md#std-017) governs the conditional deployment and operational choice; one bounded context need not become exactly one independently deployed service.

**Conditional adoption:** When a project explicitly adopts DDD for a scope, assess that scope against the expectations below. A project that selects a simpler alternative should explain its tradeoff at the architectural decision or assessment point. DDD's Recommended status does not weaken independently Required [SOLID (STD-010)](solid.md#std-010), [business-policy independence (STD-011)](dependency-direction.md#std-011), or [TDD (STD-001)](engineering-principles.md#std-001).

## Normative expectations when DDD is adopted

1. **Model business meaning with its stakeholders.** Identify the actual business decisions and constraints to be modeled. Establish terminology appropriate to the bounded context, and use it consistently in relevant conversations, scenarios, code, and documentation. Resolve materially ambiguous terms with domain expertise when accessible, or identify assumptions and unresolved questions instead of inventing business facts. No mandatory workshop format, glossary file, or large domain diagram is imposed.
2. **Identify meaningful model boundaries.** State which domain/subdomain and bounded context a model serves. Where multiple contexts interact or competing meanings exist, identify important context relationships, upstream/downstream responsibilities, integration contracts, ownership, and translation needs. Keep context-specific models separate when their semantics differ; a shared database entity or C# type does not automatically define one common domain model. Classify core, supporting, and generic subdomains when the distinction informs effort or ownership. A bounded context is a language/model boundary, not automatically a one-to-one microservice, assembly, department, or subdomain mapping.
3. **Give domain concepts appropriate identity and value semantics.** Model identity-bearing concepts as entities when continuity across changes matters. Prefer value objects with value-based equality, valid construction, and immutability where identity is irrelevant. Do not introduce base classes, marker interfaces, inheritance, or wrapper objects merely to claim a tactical pattern. A simple data record with no significant business behavior can remain a simple record or persistence representation.
4. **Protect real invariants through behavior.** Place significant domain decisions, permitted state transitions, validation, and consistency rules with the entity, value object, aggregate, or domain service that owns the rule. Avoid publicly writable state or parallel command paths that bypass a meaningful invariant. Introduce an aggregate with a root only where a genuine consistency/transaction boundary justifies it; when adopted, protect relevant mutations through that root and avoid changing aggregate children independently. Neither an aggregate per entity nor one aggregate for an entire bounded context is required. Address persistence, concurrency, and cross-aggregate guarantees separately where they are essential: in-memory invariant tests alone do not prove persisted consistency.
5. **Distinguish domain behavior from use-case coordination.** Domain services express business rules that do not naturally belong to one entity or value object. Application services and handlers coordinate use cases, transactions, authorization, and external operations; do not relocate domain rules into orchestration merely because a framework generates handler classes. Keep domain models persistence-ignorant. When policy genuinely needs an external capability, apply the consumer-oriented contracts and dependency rules owned by STD-011. Use a repository abstraction where valuable, typically around aggregate-root access if aggregates are modeled; no repository per entity/table, ORM, Unit of Work abstraction, or DI container is mandated by DDD.
6. **Use events only when their meaning and tradeoffs justify them.** Domain events describe significant business occurrences, not every data mutation. Use direct calls when simpler. When events are used, distinguish domain reactions inside a context from integration events across system/context boundaries; consider delivery, transaction ordering, duplicate handling, and post-commit publication as appropriate to the actual system. This does not mandate asynchronous messaging, eventual consistency for every interaction, CQRS, or event sourcing.
7. **Keep modeling independent of physical architecture.** A single production project, modular monolith, or distributed service can adopt DDD. Model boundaries do not require separate assemblies, deployment units, a prescribed folder hierarchy, a specific database representation, or the automatic adoption of Clean or Hexagonal Architecture. Where another style is adopted, satisfy its own independent conditions.

These are **semantic expectations for the chosen DDD scope**, not a quota of patterns. The strongest evidence is that significant modeled rules, terminology, and boundaries match known business scenarios and remain protected during legitimate operations.

## Relationship to existing authorities

| Authority | Independent responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| [STD-010 — SOLID](solid.md#std-010) | Required quality of object responsibilities, contracts, and substitutions; no mandatory interface per entity. |
| [STD-011 — Business-policy dependency direction](dependency-direction.md#std-011) | Required policy independence from concrete technical mechanisms, whether DDD is chosen or not. STD-014 does not redefine this dependency rule. |
| [STD-012 — Hexagonal](hexagonal.md#std-012) | Separately Recommended ports/adapters architecture; explicit interfaces for identified inbound and outbound ports **when Hexagonal is adopted**. |
| [STD-013 — Clean](clean.md#std-013) | Separately Recommended core-wide inward compile-time dependency direction **when Clean Architecture is adopted**. |
| [STD-003 — Separate compiled projects](engineering-principles.md#std-003) | Optional physical dependency-enforcement choice; not required by a bounded context. |
| [STD-001 — TDD](engineering-principles.md#std-001) and [STD-009 — External-boundary verification](testing.md#std-009) | Independently applicable development discipline and conditional real-provider/validated-contract evidence. |

DDD answers **whether a model accurately expresses business meaning and protects its rules**. SOLID concerns object design; STD-011 and Clean/Hexagonal Architecture concern different dependency boundaries. A system can obey those design and dependency rules while modeling the business incorrectly. Conversely, a good domain model does not excuse a forbidden policy-to-infrastructure dependency.

## Proportionate review and verification

For new or materially changed adopted DDD scopes, select evidence corresponding to actual decisions and risks:

1. **Trace meaningful scenarios to concepts.** Identify the bounded context, relevant terms, domain assumptions, changed business rules, ownership, and any adjacent model translations. Compare with domain-expert or accepted requirements evidence where available. Record unresolved interpretations honestly.
2. **Exercise domain behavior independently.** Test representative allowed operations, rejected operations, relevant state transitions, value semantics, and invariant preservation through meaningful public behavior, independently of unrelated storage/UI/network mechanisms. [STD-001](engineering-principles.md#std-001) governs the test-first sequence for executable behavior; a passing suite does not prove that chronology.
3. **Review consistency boundary realism.** Check whether aggregate membership follows actual transactional invariants, whether callers can bypass protected transitions, and what ensures relevant uniqueness, concurrency, persistence, or cross-aggregate guarantees. Add proportionate integration/transaction/concurrency tests where those real mechanisms are material. [STD-009](testing.md#std-009) separately governs significant external-adapter verification when triggered; isolated domain tests cannot establish database behavior.
4. **Review interaction semantics and technical boundaries.** Where contexts interact, inspect contracts/translation and ownership; where events are used, review domain versus integration semantics and commit/delivery expectations. Respect independent STD-011–013 constraints as applicable.
5. **Record evidence and limits.** Identify exact revisions, scenarios, artifacts, executed tests and results, semantic reviews, alternatives, and unverified behavior. A context-map picture, compiled code, namespace structure, pattern count, or green unit suite alone does **not** prove domain fidelity, correctness under concurrency, or production event reliability.

No universal DDD analyzer, mandatory domain workshop, test framework, newly compiled project, or CI gate is introduced. The [practical DDD guide](../ddd-guidance.md#level-3-review-testing-and-evolution) shows review scenarios and evidence limits; its C# illustrations are not executable templates or automatically verified examples.

## Alternatives and deviations

- **Document a simpler approach** when domain complexity is low or an existing codebase has a more economical design. Data-centric CRUD, transaction scripts with clearly managed rules, or pure functions can be appropriate. A Recommended style can be omitted with a contextual explanation; do not call that a Required-standard exception.
- **Adopt selectively and incrementally** when only some subdomains justify rich models. A bounded context may remain one logical module in an existing .NET Framework assembly. Preserve published behavior and compatibility while moving meaningful rules inward.
- **Describe an adopted-style deviation** when a DDD-labeled scope fails to use its context language consistently, exposes invariant-breaking mutations, or conflates incompatible models. State the limitation, alternative, affected behavior, and relevant follow-up instead of claiming full adoption. Independently Required standards use their own reviewed-exception process under [standards governance](../governance/standards.md#applicability-levels).
- **Avoid speculative patterns** when evidence of domain need is absent. Extra aggregate roots, repository interfaces, domain-event infrastructure, microservices, or separate assemblies can obscure the actual rules and increase maintenance costs.

## Authoritative Microsoft references

- [Use domain analysis to model microservices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/microservices/model/domain-analysis) — strategic domain/subdomain mapping, ubiquitous language, bounded contexts, ownership, and context relationships.
- [Use tactical DDD to design microservices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/microservices/model/tactical-domain-driven-design) — entities, value objects, aggregate consistency, domain/application services, and events.
- [Design a DDD-oriented microservice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/architecture/microservices/microservice-ddd-cqrs-patterns/ddd-oriented-microservice) — real business complexity versus simple CRUD and logical layered responsibilities.
- [Design a microservice domain model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/architecture/microservices/microservice-ddd-cqrs-patterns/microservice-domain-model) — rich model behavior, invariants, and aggregate-root consistency.
- [Design the infrastructure persistence layer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/architecture/microservices/microservice-ddd-cqrs-patterns/infrastructure-persistence-layer-design) — repository-per-aggregate-root when that pattern is used, while custom repositories are not essential to DDD.
- [Domain events: design and implementation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/architecture/microservices/microservice-ddd-cqrs-patterns/domain-events-design-implementation) — domain versus integration events and transaction boundaries.

These are authoritative **documented guidance**, not verification of a consuming project's implementation. Microsoft's examples often use microservices; STD-014 does not infer that their deployment-specific patterns are universally mandatory. The playbook's separate Recommended adoption and contextual assessment criteria are its approved choices.
